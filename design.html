How the workflow is built

Two routes that meet

In the agent-supervised route, an out-of-plane distorted structure passes through the agentic optimizer module at Skala-1.1/def2-SVP, and one end point per molecule is handed, unchanged, to the Hessian module and to the NMR workflow module. In the hand-set route, a strained or randomly deformed structure passes through a fixed schedule of semi-empirical, machine-learned-potential and staged Skala-1.1 passes up to def2-TZVP. The routes meet at the def2-TZVP minima, where they are compared directly (section 2.1).

Where module 4 sits

Computed in the Note on the def2-TZVP minima of the separately run hand-set route, not on output of the Hessian module. The two routes meet at def2-TZVP: 0.004 Å and 0.8 meV apart for C10H6, 0.007 Å and 3.5 meV for C11H8 (section 3.4).

One supervision layer, selectable controller

Monitor

After every step, plain code computes trajectory features over a five-step window and checks six stall triggers, among them an uphill step, oscillation, a plateau and steps pinned at the cap. Most steps raise no trigger and never reach the model.

Controller

The controller is a setting of the module: the language-model agent, which answers through function calling and always sees the recommendation of a deterministic step advisor, or a rule-based controller that makes no model call. Both pass the same validator, probation and rollback.

Guard

A validator clips out-of-range values and rejects unsupported actions: per decision the step cap may at most double (or rise to 0.02 Å, if that is larger) and never exceeds 0.05 Å. An accepted change runs for five steps from a checkpoint and is rolled back if the energy or fmax has worsened beyond set tolerances. After two rollbacks the controller is switched off.

A rule-based controller did as well where we tested it, so a language model is not shown to be required. The benefit is credited to the supervision layer: the monitor, the step advisor and the guard. L-BFGS makes every step; the controller changes settings and never writes coordinates.

Which energies

Energies are electronic energies at fixed nuclei, on the Born–Oppenheimer surface. No zero-point, thermal or entropy term is added, and no free energy is reported.

We compare energy differences, not the total energies of two functionals, which have different zero points.

Software stack

  • PySCF and GPU4PySCF for every DFT step
  • ASE L-BFGS for the optimizer
  • GFN2-xTB through tblite
  • MACE in the hand-set route
  • Python 3

So that the four modules are released on one open-source software stack (section 2.4). Versions and hardware are listed on Reproduce.
