How it works

Every Cartesian coordinate is displaced by ±0.01 Å and the Skala-1.1 forces are evaluated at each displaced structure: 6N + 1 = 97 gradients for C10H6 and 115 for C11H8. The Hessian is assembled from differences of the forces, projected free of translations and rotations, mass-weighted and diagonalized.

Frequencies are harmonic and unscaled. Dipole moments stored at every displaced geometry give double-harmonic IR intensities, and the vDOS is a sum of unit-weight Gaussians (section 2.3).

Why here

The forces vanish at a valley floor and at a mountain pass alike; only the curvature tells them apart. The Hessian module is run on a structure that has reached the force target, not at every step. The same calculation gives the harmonic frequencies, the IR intensities and the vibrational density of states (sections 2.1, 2.3 and 3.2).

Terms used here: Hessian, imaginary mode, minimum, saddle point, vDOS.

Energy scans along soft modes

We scanned the energy along the three lowest modes and one control mode of each molecule. All 64 displaced structures lay above the minimum. This check does not depend on the displacement step (section 3.2).

The two softest modes of C11H8 (89 cm−1) are strongly anharmonic: at three times the zero-point amplitude their scanned energy was 2.2 and 3.5 times the harmonic value. They are reported by sign and character only.

Limit

One displacement step (0.01 Å) and one integration grid were used, and neither was varied. The rigid-body residuals reach 55 cm−1; the frequencies are considered precise to about ±20 cm−1. Both molecules are rigid and planar; a flexible molecule with many soft modes was not examined (section 3.3).
