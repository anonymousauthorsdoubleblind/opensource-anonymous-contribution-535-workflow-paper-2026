How it works

The optimizer is L-BFGS, a standard method that moves the atoms downhill on the energy, like a ball rolling to the lowest point. The module is one block. Before the run, plain code runs a GFN2-xTB basin search from 13 seeds, and the language model chooses the starting basin from the resulting table. During the run, L-BFGS on a Skala-1.1 calculator makes every geometry step, in a schedule of passes that each carry a step cap, a force target, an SCF tolerance and a step budget; these settings can change between any two steps without restarting the optimizer. At the end, the model checks the structure, the bonds and the planarity (section 2.2).

Monitor

After every step, plain code computes trajectory features over a five-step window and checks six stall triggers, among them an uphill step, oscillation, a plateau and steps pinned at the cap. Most steps raise no trigger and never reach the model.

Controller

The controller is a setting of the module: the language-model agent, which answers through function calling and always sees the recommendation of a deterministic step advisor, or a rule-based controller that makes no model call. Both pass the same validator, probation and rollback.

Guard

A validator clips out-of-range values and rejects unsupported actions: per decision the step cap may at most double (or rise to 0.02 Å, if that is larger) and never exceeds 0.05 Å. An accepted change runs for five steps from a checkpoint and is rolled back if the energy or fmax has worsened beyond set tolerances. After two rollbacks the controller is switched off.

What the model cannot do

It never writes coordinates and never computes an energy or a force, and free text is never parsed into an action. Every decision ends with exactly one action from a closed vocabulary of eight; four were used on the annulenes.

Terms used here: step cap, L-BFGS, controller, guard, probation, supervision layer.

Step-cap sweep: small multiples

We started runs on purpose with a step cap of 0.005 Å, forty times below the ASE default. Every arm used start R1, Skala-1.1/def2-SVP, L-BFGS memory 20 and a warm-started SCF, so the module is the only difference between the plain and the supervised arm. Plain L-BFGS was held at the cap on 100% (C10H6) and 95% (C11H8) of its steps. For C10H6 it did not reach the force target within 149 steps; for C11H8 it reached it at step 129. The supervised runs raised the cap from step 6 onward, were held at it on only 30 to 40% of their steps, and reached the target at steps 60 and 58 (C10H6) and 70 and 70 (C11H8). A rules run on C10H6, with the rule-based controller in place of the agent, reached the target at step 50 (section 3.6).

Limit

The cap was mis-set on purpose. At the ASE defaults (step cap 0.2 Å, memory 100) a plain run on C10H6 reached the target in 48 steps, and the cap never limited a step. Supervision guards against a poor setting; it does not improve on a good one. With a sensible cap of 0.025 Å the two arms tied: 50 against 53 steps, and 63 against 65. With a cap of 0.3 Å the first step destroyed the C11H8 structure in all three runs; the module cannot undo this. Single runs, given as step counts. No gain in speed is claimed. The rules run is a single run on one molecule; a difference of 8 to 10 steps is not evidence that either controller is better. Its end point lay 0.006 Å and 0.3 meV from that of the supervised run.

Limit

The decisions depend on the version of the model and are not reproducible bit for bit; for this reason every prompt, call and decision is released. The agent agreed with the deterministic step advisor in 81 of the 97 grid decisions that had a recommendation, so these runs do not show what the language model adds beyond the advisor. The trap-escape actions and the choice of basin were not exercised on the annulenes.

The ammonia saddle test

Which part is which

Energies, forces, step caps, probe results and decisions are logged. The geometry between the logged start and end structures was not stored per step; the picture places N at the height that the relaxed inversion profile gives for the logged energy.

At a saddle point the forces vanish, as they do at a minimum, yet the energy still falls in one direction. We ran ten plain L-BFGS optimizations of ammonia at Skala-1.1/def2-SVP, six from the exact planar saddle point and four from two slightly displaced structures. Eight ended on the saddle point with the force target met. The saddle point lies 323 meV above the minimum and has one imaginary mode near −910 cm−1. In six supervised runs the probe reported an imaginary mode seven times; each time the module made a bounded displacement along that mode, and all six runs ended in a minimum, confirmed by a finite-difference Hessian. On hydrogen peroxide, whose planar trans form is a saddle point 0.58 kcal mol−1 (25 meV) above the skew minimum with one imaginary mode near −244 cm−1, three plain runs stopped on the saddle point after 1, 7 and 6 steps, and three supervised runs each made one bounded displacement and ended in a minimum (section 3.5).

Limit

The test molecules are ammonia and hydrogen peroxide, not annulenes, and a compact version of the module without the xTB stage was used. Five of the six supervised ammonia runs were first attempts; the sixth followed a correction to the test harness. A compact rule-based supervisor without a language model also ended in a minimum in every run: six on ammonia and three on hydrogen peroxide. So the test shows that the probe and the bounded displacement work; it does not show that a language model is needed. In this test the model answered through structured JSON output, not native function calling. On the two annulenes the probe met no imaginary mode.

Different starts and settings end at one structure

Of the 18 supervised runs, 16 reached the force target: nine for C10H6 and seven for C11H8. Their end points differed by at most 0.016 Å (C10H6) and 0.028 Å (C11H8) in pairwise RMSD, and their energies by at most 1.7 meV. Re-optimized at def2-TZVP, they lay 0.004 Å and 0.8 meV (C10H6) and 0.007 Å and 3.5 meV (C11H8) from the structure of the separately run hand-set schedule (section 3.4).

Main-grid runs are single runs of two set-ups that also differ in L-BFGS memory and warm start (supervised: memory 20, warm start; plain: memory 1, cold start). Their step counts are not the effect of the agent alone and no speed-up is claimed from them. The like-for-like test is the step-cap sweep.

Limit

Both routes share the functional and the L-BFGS family of optimizers, so this tests the path and the settings, not the functional. Two pairs of repeated runs were identical, so the 16 runs are not 16 independent samples. One planar basin was found for each molecule. In the main grid the two set-ups differ in memory, warm start and supervision together.

When to use it

For these two molecules the results suggest a simple rule. With a sensible starting cap near 0.025 Å, or at the ASE defaults, plain L-BFGS is sufficient. Supervision pays when a good cap is not known in advance. The rule-based controller needs no model call. The module does not rescue a run whose first step wrecks the structure (section 5).
