Why this step matters

New drugs, batteries and fertilizers begin as molecules nobody has made. Researchers first simulate many candidates on a computer and send only a short list to the laboratory. This route is called high-throughput in-silico screening. Skala-1.1 is a neural-network exchange–correlation functional that is reported to match expensive hybrid functionals at the cost of simple semi-local ones. This Note takes up one step of the route: the step that turns a rough candidate structure into a verified one. Screening itself is not demonstrated here (sections 1.1 and 1.2).

Four needs, four modules

Module 1

Agentic optimizer module

(i) Optimize the structure to a minimum, with optimizer settings that adapt during the run.

Why this compute, here. Too small a cap makes the optimizer creep; too large a cap can wreck the structure. With a neural functional, each wasted step is one expensive gradient. Settings are therefore adapted during the run, without a restart, and behind a guard that bounds each change and reverses it if the run gets worse (sections 1.2 and 2.2).

Module 2

Hessian module

(ii) Confirm that the end point is a minimum.

Why this compute, here. The forces vanish at a valley floor and at a mountain pass alike; only the curvature tells them apart. The Hessian module is run on a structure that has reached the force target, not at every step (sections 2.1, 2.3 and 3.2).

Open the Hessian module page

Module 3

NMR workflow module

(iii) Show that it agrees with what the laboratory measures.

Why this compute, here. An NMR spectrum is a fingerprint of the structure that a laboratory can measure. C11H8 is a liquid at room temperature, so no crystal structure exists and the NMR spectra are the primary experimental reference (sections 1.2, 2.4 and 3.1).

Open the NMR workflow module page

Module 4

Molecular property module

(iv) Compute molecular properties that an established method can check.

Why this compute, here. A verified structure is the starting point for the quantities a chemist uses: where the electronic charge sits and how much energy a distortion costs. An established hybrid functional and CCSD(T) give a second opinion (section 3.8).

Four gaps in the literature

All four statements are made to our knowledge. They rest on searches of Google Scholar, arXiv, ChemRxiv, the Skala repository and web search, run on 10 to 13 November 2026.

1 · No harmonic frequencies, infrared intensities or vibrational densities of states of an annulene computed with Skala-1.1 have been published.

2 · No Skala-optimized annulene geometry has been tested against the experimental ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectrum of the synthesized compound.

3 · To our knowledge, no earlier work has a large language model agent, through function calling with typed actions, supervise the step and convergence settings of a running Skala-1.1/L-BFGS geometry optimization behind a validator, a probation window and rollback of the running optimization.

4 · No publication independently evaluates Skala's release of high steric strain (more than 1 eV) in an annulene geometry optimization.

Searches of 10 to 13 November 2026.

Four limits on these statements

  1. Adapting the step size during a run is standard practice in optimizers, and rule-based supervision of a run is not claimed as new.
  2. Most elements of the third gap have precedent on their own, so that statement rests on their combination.
  3. The third statement says what has not been reported; it does not say that a language model is needed for the supervision, which the Note does not show.
  4. Geometry optimization with Skala-1.1 without an agent is already supported through ASE and GPU4PySCF.

The searches were bounded, so the absence of earlier work in them is not proof that none exists.

What the Note contributes

A workflow of four open-source modules that takes a rough, strained input structure to a verified Skala-1.1 minimum and to quantities a laboratory can measure, validated on two laboratory-synthesized annulenes. For the optimizer, the contribution is an interface in which every change is a typed, bounded, reversible and logged action, which any controller, rule-based or model-based, can use. We attribute the benefit to the supervision layer, that is, the monitor, the step advisor and the guard, and not to the language model (section 5).

A rule-based controller did as well where we tested it, so a language model is not shown to be required.

What is not claimed

  • No speed-up.
  • Not the accuracy of Skala-1.1 in general, and not its cost relative to conventional functionals.
  • Not that a language model is needed for the supervision: a rule-based controller did as well where we tested it.
  • Not NMR with Skala-1.1, and not a ranking of functionals.
  • No free energies, populations or reaction thermochemistry.
  • No screening or discovery.
  • No transfer to larger, flexible or heteroatom-containing molecules.

Why these molecules

Dehydro[10]annulenes are strained, planar and fully conjugated. Both C11H8 and the parent C10H6 have been synthesized, and both have published ¹H and ¹³C NMR spectra. C11H8 is a liquid at room temperature, so no crystal structure exists; the NMR spectra are therefore the primary experimental reference (section 1.2).
