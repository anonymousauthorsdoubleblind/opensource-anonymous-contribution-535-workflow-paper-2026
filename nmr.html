How it works

The comparison follows the structure-assignment protocol of Willoughby, Jansma and Hoye, which prescribes B3LYP/6-311+G(2d,p) shieldings on M06-2X/6-31+G(d,p) geometries. We depart from it in two ways: the geometry comes from Skala-1.1 in place of M06-2X, and no continuum solvent model was used. The scaling constants have not been fitted on Skala-1.1 geometries, so they are used outside their calibrated functional, though not outside their calibrated basis. No parameter was fitted to the annulenes, and atom labels were assigned from connectivity, never from computed shifts (section 2.4).

Why here

An NMR spectrum is a fingerprint of the structure that a laboratory can measure. C11H8 is a liquid at room temperature, so no crystal structure exists and the NMR spectra are the primary experimental reference. The shifts are computed on the Skala-1.1 geometry: this tests the geometry, not NMR with Skala-1.1 (sections 1.2, 2.4 and 3.1).

Terms used here: GIAO, MAE.

The ¹³C MAE was 1.30 to 1.95 ppm and the ¹H MAE 0.08 to 0.17 ppm. All errors fell below the limits recommended by Parmar et al. (2.0 ppm for ¹³C, 0.2 ppm for ¹H). All 24 resolvable signal pairs appeared in the measured order (section 3.1).

Limit

The shieldings come from B3LYP and PBE0, not from Skala-1.1. This tests the geometry, not NMR with Skala-1.1. It does not rank Skala-1.1 against other functionals. The alkyne carbons of C10H6 were 3.3 ppm (P1) and 4.9 ppm (P2) too high; weighted by nucleus, the ¹³C error of C10H6 would rise to 2.1 ppm, slightly above the limit. The shieldings refer to the gas phase. Only 3 of the 24 pairs lie within twice the criterion, so the signal order is a necessary condition, not a discriminating one. Three of the four ¹H MAEs exceed the 0.10 ppm that the protocol paper gives as typical of correct fits (section 5.1).

Where the errors lie

The largest ¹³C errors of C11H8 were at its sp³ carbon C6 (+1.92 and +2.83 ppm); those of C10H6 were at its four alkyne carbons. C10H6 is the weaker test: its D2h symmetry leaves only four resolvable pairs, and its spectra were recorded at −90 and −75 °C. The computed protons lay on average 0.03 to 0.17 ppm below experiment, which is consistent with gas-phase shieldings and constants fitted in a chloroform continuum model.
